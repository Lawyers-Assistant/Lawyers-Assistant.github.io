Welcome to the Legal Desk — Notes for the Modern Practitioner

Why this blog exists, what you'll find here, and how these notes are written — plainly, for lawyers who value their time and their craft.

This is the first note on the Legal Desk, a place for the kind of writing lawyers actually need: short, plain, and practical. No filler, no marketing, no ten-paragraph throat-clearing. Just the craft of law, written down clearly.

What you'll find here

Every article falls into one of a few honest categories:

  • Practice notes — how to run a file, manage a matter, or handle the day-to-day realities of practice.
  • Legal research — technique: finding, reading, and reasoning from authority, and verifying before you rely.
  • Legal writing — drafting that persuades, from memos to submissions, with examples worth stealing.
  • The profession — the wider questions: technology, ethics, and the changing shape of legal work.

How these notes are written

Three rules govern everything here:

  1. Say it once, say it plainly. The first draft should be the last draft as often as possible.
  2. Cite what you rely on. Where a rule or case matters, it gets named — so you can check the work.
  3. Respect the reader's time. If a note can be three paragraphs, it is not stretched to five.

"The practice of law is the discipline of being useful — precisely, ethically, and on time."

If you practice law, or work alongside those who do, pull up a chair. The next note will be worth your time — or it won't be published.
